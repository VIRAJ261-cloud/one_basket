    How it works

    A short, non-technical tour of what happens when you press Optimize.

    1 · Delivery-area check

    Each order is checked first: is this restaurant allowed to deliver to that society, and is the trip short enough? Orders that fail are listed with the reason and are never given to a driver.

    2 · Shortest roads (Dijkstra / A*)

    Dijkstra finds the quickest road path between two places by always extending the closest unexplored place first. A* finds the same path but uses the straight-line distance to the goal to search in the right direction first.

    3 · Multi-stop optimizer

    Orders are added one at a time where they add the least extra driving, without breaking a rule: food is collected before it is delivered, a driver never carries more than their limit, and food does not ride around too long. Orders not yet collected may then move to a better driver.

    4 · Simple route for comparison

    The "simple route" handles one order at a time: collect, deliver, then the next order. It uses the same drivers and rules, so the two plans can be compared fairly.

    5 · Rerouting

    When a road is blocked, it is removed from the map. Drivers already on a road finish that stretch, then follow a new path. If a place can no longer be reached, the affected orders are reported instead of being marked delivered.

    Good to know

    The optimized route is a good plan found by a fast method, not a guaranteed perfect one. Locations are placed by their real latitude/longitude, but roads are drawn as straight lines between connected places, not street shapes. Delivery-area rules and limits are project assumptions.
